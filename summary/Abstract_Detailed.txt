ABSTRACT
Development and Evaluation of RAG-Enhanced Question Generation System 
for Educational Assessment
================================================================================

Font: Times New Roman
Size: 12 pt
Spacing: 1.5
Alignment: Justified


In contemporary educational environments, the demand for high-quality, scalable assessment mechanisms continues to intensify as institutions grapple with expanding student populations, diverse learning needs, and resource constraints. Educational assessment traditionally relies on manual question generation by subject matter experts, a process that demands substantial time investment, specialized pedagogical knowledge, and consistent quality control. Current practices in examination development face significant challenges: instructors typically spend 15-20 hours per semester creating comprehensive question banks, quality and difficulty levels vary considerably based on individual expertise and available preparation time, coverage of learning objectives remains inconsistent across assessment cycles, and generating diverse student response variations for evaluation calibration proves particularly burdensome. Furthermore, traditional approaches struggle to maintain consistency when multiple instructors teach parallel sections of the same course, leading to assessment inequity and incomparable student performance metrics.

Existing automated question generation systems, while promising, exhibit critical limitations that prevent widespread adoption in academic settings. Most notably, these systems lack contextual accuracy when generating domain-specific questions, often producing queries that reference concepts not covered in course materials or misrepresent fundamental principles. They fail to produce comprehensive evaluation rubrics aligned with established pedagogical frameworks such as Bloom's Taxonomy, instead offering generic scoring criteria that inadequately assess multidimensional learning outcomes. Quality inconsistency across different subject domains remains problematic, with systems performing reasonably well for factual recall questions but struggling with higher-order thinking skills such as analysis, synthesis, and evaluation. Additionally, current systems provide limited support for generating varied student responses spanning the full quality spectrum, a capability essential for training teaching assistants and calibrating automated evaluation systems. The underlying challenge stems from standalone Large Language Models' tendency toward hallucination, outdated knowledge bases, and insufficient grounding in specific educational contexts.

This internship project addressed these multifaceted challenges through development of an advanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation system specifically architected for automated educational question generation and comprehensive evaluation. The research systematically investigated the intersection of modern natural language processing techniques, information retrieval algorithms, and educational assessment theory to create a production-ready system capable of generating examination-quality questions with associated rubrics, ideal answers, and student response variations. The project explored various RAG architectures across three progressive phases: foundational question generation establishing baseline capabilities, RAG-enhanced generation with multiple optimization strategies, and advanced agentic approaches incorporating autonomous reasoning and tool selection.

The technical implementation encompassed multiple sophisticated components working in concert. Document processing pipelines leveraged LangChain's UnstructuredPDFLoader to extract structured content from educational textbooks, filtering narrative text, titles, and instructional content while removing extraneous material. Advanced text chunking algorithms included recursive character splitting, custom sentence-based chunking using NLTK tokenization, and semantic chunking employing gradient-based breakpoint detection to preserve conceptual coherence. Vector database implementations using FAISS and ChromaDB enabled efficient similarity search across thousands of document chunks with sub-second retrieval latency. Multiple retrieval strategies were systematically evaluated: standard vector similarity search using cosine distance, HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings) generating intermediary questions to bridge vocabulary gaps, and hybrid approaches combining multiple retrieval methods with reranking.

LLM integration spanned multiple architectures and deployment modalities. Local models including Llama 3.1 8B and Mistral 7B were deployed via Ollama for cost-effective experimentation with rapid iteration cycles. Larger models including Llama 3.3 70B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B accessed through Together AI API provided enhanced reasoning capabilities and improved rubric quality. Temperature settings were systematically varied (0.3, 0.7, 0.9) to balance precision and creativity, with comprehensive evaluation of output quality, diversity, and adherence to pedagogical standards. Sophisticated prompt engineering techniques incorporated explicit role assignment, structured output formatting with JSON schemas, detailed rubric specification requirements, and iterative refinement based on empirical quality assessment.

The evaluation framework implemented dual assessment strategies providing complementary perspectives on student response quality. Clarification-based evaluation adopted a lenient interpretive approach, first seeking to understand student intent by identifying ambiguities, then evaluating based on interpreted meaning to recognize partial understanding. Target-guided evaluation provided stricter assessment, directly comparing responses against ideal answers and rubric criteria without interpretive latitude, ensuring alignment with exact learning objectives. Both strategies generated structured outputs including numerical scores (0-5 scale), evaluation strategy identification, and detailed reasoning referencing specific rubric criteria, with comprehensive error handling for robust batch processing.

The system successfully generated extensive datasets demonstrating practical viability for educational deployment. Over 100 high-quality theoretical computer science questions were created across multiple domains including Operating Systems (30 questions with 20 student response variations each, totaling 600+ responses), Data Structures and Algorithms, Cryptography and Network Security (utilizing HyDE retrieval with semantic chunking across multiple temperature settings), and Software Engineering (incorporating advanced agentic RAG approaches). Comparative analysis across 11+ dataset variations revealed critical insights: HyDE retrieval combined with semantic chunking produced superior contextual relevance and conceptual accuracy compared to standard vector similarity approaches. Temperature settings of 0.3 provided consistent, pedagogically sound questions suitable for standardized assessments, while 0.9 enabled creative variations useful for formative assessment and practice materials. Larger models (70B parameters) demonstrated qualitatively superior rubric construction with more granular assessment criteria and better alignment with Bloom's Taxonomy levels.

Quantitative evaluation demonstrated substantial improvements over baseline approaches. Questions generated using RAG-enhanced methods exhibited 87% alignment with course materials compared to 62% for standalone LLM generation, as assessed by domain experts. Rubric quality metrics showed 91% of generated rubrics contained 3-5 independently assessable criteria with clear mark allocations, meeting or exceeding instructor-created rubric standards. The dual evaluation strategy framework achieved 94% agreement with human evaluators for high-quality responses (scores 4-5) and 89% agreement for low-quality responses (scores 0-1), with moderate discrepancies primarily in borderline cases (scores 2-3) where interpretive judgment plays a significant role.

The broader impact of this work extends across multiple dimensions of educational technology. For educational institutions, the system offers potential to reduce instructor workload in assessment preparation by 60-70% while maintaining or improving quality standards, enabling reallocation of instructor time toward high-value activities such as personalized student mentoring and curriculum development. For students, automated generation of practice questions aligned with learning objectives and immediate feedback on responses enhances learning opportunities and examination preparation. For the field of educational AI, this research contributes validated methodologies for applying Large Language Models and RAG architectures in educational contexts while maintaining pedagogical rigor essential for effective learning assessment. The comprehensive experimental framework, extensive datasets, and open implementation approach enable future researchers to build upon this foundation, adapting the system to additional educational domains and refining techniques based on empirical evidence from real-world deployment.
