INTERNSHIP REPORT

Development and Evaluation of RAG-Enhanced Question Generation System for Educational Assessment


================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

Educational assessment has traditionally relied on manual question generation by instructors, a time-intensive process prone to inconsistencies and limited by individual expertise. Current automated question generation systems suffer from several limitations: they lack contextual accuracy when generating domain-specific questions, fail to produce comprehensive evaluation rubrics aligned with pedagogical standards, and demonstrate inconsistent quality across different subject domains. Furthermore, existing systems struggle to generate varied student responses for evaluation calibration and provide limited support for multi-dimensional assessment criteria.

This internship project addressed these critical challenges by developing an advanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system specifically designed for automated educational question generation and evaluation. The research explored various RAG architectures, chunking strategies, and Large Language Model (LLM) configurations to optimize question quality and contextual relevance. The project systematically investigated multiple retrieval techniques including HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings), semantic chunking, and paragraph-level chunking, combined with different embedding models and vector store implementations.

The proposed solution integrates multiple components: PDF document processing for extracting educational content, advanced text chunking algorithms optimized for educational material, vector database implementations using FAISS and ChromaDB for efficient information retrieval, and LLM-based generation using models including Llama 3.1 8B, Llama 3.3 70B, DeepSeek-R1, and Mistral 7B. The system implements sophisticated prompt engineering techniques to generate examination-quality questions with detailed rubrics, ideal answers, and multiple student response variations for comprehensive evaluation.

The outcomes demonstrate significant improvements in automated educational assessment. The system successfully generated over 30 high-quality theoretical questions across multiple computer science domains including Operating Systems, Data Structures and Algorithms, Cryptography and Network Security, and Software Engineering. Comparative analysis revealed that temperature settings, retrieval strategies, and chunking methods significantly impact question quality and diversity. The HyDE approach with semantic chunking produced superior contextual relevance, while temperature variations of 0.3 and 0.9 balanced precision and creativity. The evaluation framework successfully assessed student responses using multiple strategies including clarification-based and target-guided evaluation, providing detailed scoring and reasoning aligned with educational rubrics. This internship project establishes a comprehensive framework for AI-assisted educational assessment, reducing instructor workload while maintaining pedagogical rigor and enabling scalable, consistent evaluation processes across diverse educational contexts.


================================================================================
INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

The landscape of educational technology is experiencing rapid transformation as artificial intelligence and natural language processing capabilities advance. Educational institutions worldwide face increasing demands to provide high-quality assessments while managing growing student populations and diverse learning needs. Traditional manual question generation and evaluation methods, while effective, require substantial time investment and are difficult to scale consistently across large cohorts.

This internship was conducted as part of an academic research initiative focused on exploring the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and educational assessment. The internship spanned approximately three weeks during which comprehensive research, implementation, and evaluation were conducted across multiple phases.

The motivation behind this project stems from several key observations in educational assessment. Instructors spend considerable time crafting examination questions that accurately assess learning outcomes while maintaining appropriate difficulty levels and comprehensive coverage of course materials. The quality and consistency of assessments can vary significantly based on individual instructor expertise and available time. Furthermore, generating diverse student response variations for evaluation calibration and teaching assistant training represents an additional burden on educational staff.

Recent advances in Large Language Models such as GPT, Llama, and other transformer-based architectures have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding context, generating coherent text, and reasoning about complex topics. However, standalone LLMs often suffer from hallucination, outdated knowledge, and lack of domain-specific accuracy. Retrieval-Augmented Generation addresses these limitations by grounding LLM responses in relevant retrieved documents, thereby improving factual accuracy and contextual relevance.

This project specifically investigated how RAG systems can be optimized for educational question generation by exploring various document processing techniques, retrieval strategies, embedding models, and generation parameters. The research examined three critical phases: foundational question generation without retrieval augmentation, RAG-enhanced question generation with multiple optimization strategies, and comprehensive evaluation frameworks for assessing both generated questions and student responses.

The domain context includes Computer Science education at the university level, with specific focus on theoretical subjects including Operating Systems, Data Structures and Algorithms, Cryptography and Network Security, and Software Engineering. These subjects were chosen because they represent core computer science curricula and include substantial conceptual knowledge that benefits from thorough assessment beyond programming exercises.


================================================================================
INTERNSHIP AIMS AND OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

The primary aim of this internship was to develop and validate a comprehensive Retrieval-Augmented Generation system capable of automatically generating high-quality educational questions, evaluation rubrics, and student response variations while maintaining pedagogical rigor and contextual accuracy.

Primary Objectives:

1. Investigate and implement foundational question generation capabilities using Large Language Models without retrieval augmentation to establish baseline performance metrics and understand intrinsic LLM capabilities for educational content generation.

2. Design and implement multiple RAG architectures incorporating various document processing strategies, text chunking algorithms, embedding models, and vector store implementations to optimize information retrieval for educational content.

3. Conduct systematic experimentation with different retrieval techniques including standard vector similarity search, HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings), semantic chunking, and paragraph-level chunking to identify optimal strategies for educational question generation.

4. Evaluate the impact of LLM hyperparameters, specifically temperature settings, on question quality, diversity, creativity, and adherence to educational standards across multiple models including Llama 3.1 8B, Llama 3.3 70B, DeepSeek-R1, and Mistral 7B.

5. Develop comprehensive evaluation frameworks capable of assessing student responses using multiple evaluation strategies including clarification-based and target-guided approaches, providing detailed scoring aligned with rubric criteria.

6. Generate substantial datasets of computer science examination questions across multiple domains with associated rubrics, ideal answers, and student response variations to validate system performance and support future research.

7. Implement agentic RAG approaches that incorporate reasoning loops, tool selection, and iterative refinement to explore advanced AI-assisted educational assessment capabilities.

Secondary Objectives:

1. Compare and contrast different embedding models including HuggingFace all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B to understand their impact on retrieval quality for educational content.

2. Analyze the trade-offs between different vector database implementations including FAISS and ChromaDB in terms of performance, scalability, and retrieval accuracy.

3. Document best practices for prompt engineering in educational contexts, specifically for question generation, rubric creation, and evaluation assessment.

4. Create reproducible experimental frameworks and code repositories that enable future researchers to build upon this work and adapt the system to additional educational domains.

Expected Outcomes:

The internship aimed to produce a validated, production-ready system capable of reducing instructor workload by 60-70% for question generation tasks while maintaining or exceeding the quality standards of manually created assessments. The expected deliverables included comprehensive datasets containing hundreds of generated questions across multiple subjects, detailed evaluation reports comparing different RAG configurations, and reusable code implementations with clear documentation.

Relevance to Industry and Academics:

This work holds significant relevance for educational technology companies developing AI-powered learning management systems, assessment platforms, and adaptive learning tools. Academic institutions can leverage these findings to improve assessment consistency, reduce instructor workload, and provide personalized learning experiences. The research contributes to the growing body of knowledge on practical applications of Large Language Models in education, specifically addressing the critical challenge of maintaining pedagogical quality while leveraging AI automation.

RESEARCH PROCESS AND IMPLEMENTATION
================================================================================

The internship followed a structured three-phase methodology, progressing from foundational exploration to advanced implementation and comprehensive evaluation. Each phase built upon insights gained from previous stages, enabling iterative refinement of the system architecture and performance.


Phase 1: Foundational Question Generation (Week 1)

The first phase established baseline capabilities by implementing direct LLM-based question generation without retrieval augmentation. This phase focused on understanding intrinsic LLM capabilities, limitations, and prompt engineering requirements for educational content.

Implementation Details:

The system was built using LangChain framework for LLM orchestration, integrating with Ollama for local model deployment. The initial implementation used Llama 3.1 8B as the primary generation model due to its balance between performance and computational requirements. A comprehensive prompt template was developed that instructed the LLM to act as an expert college-level Computer Science educator, generating theoretical questions suitable for written examinations.

The prompt engineering process involved multiple iterations to optimize output quality. Key elements included explicit instructions to generate questions from specific subjects (initially Operating Systems), guidelines requiring theoretical rather than programming-based questions, specification of 3-5 independently assessable rubric criteria, and JSON formatting requirements to ensure structured output for downstream processing.

The system implemented robust JSON parsing with error handling to address common LLM output issues including escaped characters, malformed JSON, and inconsistent formatting. A cleaning function using regular expressions extracted JSON objects from potentially noisy LLM responses. The implementation generated batches of 10-30 questions, storing them in structured JSON format with fields for question text, ideal answer, and rubric criteria.

Experimental Variables:

Temperature settings of 0.3, 0.7, and 0.9 were tested to understand the trade-off between deterministic, focused responses and creative, diverse outputs. Lower temperatures (0.3) produced more consistent, conventional questions suitable for standard assessments, while higher temperatures (0.9) generated more creative and varied questions with occasional quality inconsistencies.

Student Response Generation:

A critical innovation in this phase was the generation of diverse student response variations for each question. This capability addresses the challenge of calibrating evaluation systems and training teaching assistants. The system generated 20 student responses per question, spanning the full quality spectrum from completely incorrect to partially correct to fully correct responses. This dataset (SRIP_os_30q_20answers.json) provided essential training and validation data for subsequent evaluation system development.

Evaluation Framework Development:

The evaluation system was implemented using LM Studio's OpenAI-compatible API endpoint, enabling testing of multiple models including DeepSeek-R1 and Mistral 7B. Two distinct evaluation strategies were implemented:

Clarification-based evaluation: This approach first seeks to understand the student's intent by identifying ambiguities or unclear statements, then evaluates based on interpreted meaning. This strategy is more lenient and considers partial understanding.

Target-guided evaluation: This approach directly compares the student response against the ideal answer and rubric criteria without interpretive leniency, providing stricter assessment aligned with exact requirements.

Both strategies generated structured JSON output including a numerical score (0-5), the evaluation strategy name, and detailed reasoning referencing specific rubric items. The system handled JSON parsing errors gracefully, logging warnings for problematic responses while continuing batch processing. Results were exported to both JSON and Excel formats for comprehensive analysis.

Outcomes and Insights:

Phase 1 successfully generated over 30 high-quality Operating Systems questions with comprehensive rubrics and 600+ student response variations. Key insights included the recognition that standalone LLMs occasionally generated questions referencing concepts not covered in target educational materials, demonstrating the need for retrieval augmentation. Temperature settings significantly impacted output diversity, with 0.3 providing consistency and 0.9 enabling creative variation. The evaluation framework demonstrated that different assessment strategies could yield score variations of 1-2 points, highlighting the importance of consistent evaluation criteria.


Phase 2: RAG-Enhanced Question Generation (Week 2)

The second phase implemented comprehensive RAG architectures to ground question generation in specific educational materials, ensuring contextual accuracy and alignment with course content.

Document Processing Pipeline:

The system implemented sophisticated PDF processing using LangChain's UnstructuredPDFLoader with configuration for structured element extraction. The loader was configured with "elements" mode for structured block extraction, "fast" strategy for layout-aware parsing, and language specification for OCR accuracy. Raw documents were filtered to extract relevant page ranges corresponding to specific chapters, focusing initial experiments on Cryptography and Network Security textbook content (pages 27-44).

Content filtering retained only narrative text, titles, and list items while excluding headers, footers, page numbers, and other non-instructional content. Text cleaning removed excessive whitespace and newline characters to improve chunking quality and retrieval relevance.

Text Chunking Strategies:

Multiple chunking approaches were systematically evaluated:

Recursive Character Splitting: The baseline approach used RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter with chunk size of 400 characters and overlap of 100 characters. This method splits text hierarchically using separators (\n\n, \n, space) to maintain logical coherence. Additional experiments tested configurations with chunk sizes of 300, 400, and 500 characters with varying overlap percentages.

Semantic Chunking: This advanced approach used SemanticChunker from LangChain Experimental, which groups semantically related sentences rather than splitting at arbitrary character boundaries. The implementation used gradient-based breakpoint detection with threshold of 90.0, buffer size of 2 sentences for context, and minimum chunk size of 300 characters to prevent excessive fragmentation. This method produced more coherent chunks aligned with conceptual boundaries.

Sentence-Based Chunking: A custom SentenceSplitter class was implemented using NLTK's sentence tokenizer. This approach grouped 5 sentences per chunk with 1-sentence overlap, providing natural linguistic boundaries while maintaining reasonable chunk sizes.

Vector Database Implementation:

Multiple vector store implementations were deployed and evaluated:

FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search): Implemented for efficient similarity search with save/load capabilities for persistence. FAISS demonstrated excellent performance for smaller datasets (hundreds to thousands of chunks) with fast retrieval times and low memory overhead.

ChromaDB: Deployed for persistent storage with built-in metadata support and collection management. ChromaDB provided superior scalability for larger datasets and easier integration with metadata filtering.

Embedding Models:

HuggingFace all-MiniLM-L6-v2: The baseline embedding model, providing 384-dimensional dense vectors with good general-purpose performance and fast inference.

Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B: Advanced embedding model tested in Phase 3, providing higher-dimensional representations with improved semantic understanding at the cost of increased computational requirements.

Retrieval Strategies:

Standard Vector Similarity: Baseline retrieval using cosine similarity between query embeddings and document embeddings, retrieving top-k most similar chunks (k=3, 4, or 5).

HyDE (Hypothetical Document Embeddings): This advanced technique first generates hypothetical questions about the topic using the LLM, then uses those questions as queries to retrieve relevant documents. The implementation generated 5 hypothetical questions per topic, retrieved top-4 chunks per question, then deduplicated and ranked results. HyDE significantly improved retrieval relevance by bridging the vocabulary gap between student questions and document content.

Question Generation with RAG:

The question generation prompt was enhanced to incorporate retrieved context. The system built context strings from retrieved document chunks, then passed them to the LLM along with instructions to generate questions strictly based on the provided material. This grounding mechanism dramatically reduced hallucination and ensured questions aligned with actual course content.

Multiple model configurations were tested:

Llama 3.1 8B (local via Ollama): Primary model, temperature 0.3 and 0.9
Llama 3.3 70B (via Together AI API): Larger model for improved quality, temperature 0.3
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B (via Together AI API): Reasoning-optimized model, temperature 0.3
Mistral 7B (local via Ollama): Alternative architecture for comparison

Dataset Generation:

Phase 2 produced extensive datasets with naming convention indicating model, temperature, chapter, version, and configuration. Examples include:

- llama3_1_8b_temp0_3_chapter1_v1_hyde.json: HyDE approach with Llama 3.1 8B at temperature 0.3
- llama3_1_8b_temp0_9_chapter1_v3_hyde_para_chunking_k_per_q_4num_hyde_qs_5.json: HyDE with paragraph chunking, k=4 per query, 5 hypothetical questions
- meta-llama_Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct-Turbo-Free_temp0_3_chapter1_v3.json: 70B model results

Each dataset contained 10-30 questions with detailed rubrics structured as criterion descriptions and mark allocations.

Outcomes and Insights:

Phase 2 successfully demonstrated that RAG significantly improved question quality, contextual relevance, and alignment with course materials. HyDE retrieval outperformed standard vector similarity, particularly for conceptual questions requiring understanding of relationships between topics. Semantic chunking produced superior results compared to character-based splitting for educational materials due to better preservation of conceptual coherence. Larger models (70B) demonstrated improved rubric quality and question sophistication, though with increased latency and cost. The optimal configuration for balancing quality and efficiency was identified as Llama 3.1 8B with HyDE retrieval, semantic chunking, temperature 0.3, and k=4-5 retrieved chunks.


Phase 3: Advanced RAG and Agentic Approaches (Week 3)

The final phase explored advanced RAG architectures including agentic systems with reasoning loops, tool selection, and iterative refinement. This phase also extended question generation to additional domains including Software Engineering.

Agentic RAG Architecture:

The agentic approach introduced a multi-component system:

Query Planner/Router: Decomposes complex queries into sub-questions and determines optimal tools for each sub-task.

Tool Chest: Collection of specialized tools including vector search retriever for knowledge base queries, graph database query engine for relationship-based questions, web search API for current information, and code interpreter for technical examples.

Reasoning Loop: Core agent logic that executes plans, observes results, and decides on next steps. This iterative process continues until sufficient information is gathered or a confidence threshold is reached.

Synthesizer: Final component that aggregates information from multiple tool executions and generates coherent final answers.

Implementation used LangChain's AgentExecutor with ReAct (Reasoning + Acting) pattern, enabling the system to reason about which tools to use and when to stop iterating.

Enhanced Document Processing:

The system was extended to process Software Engineering textbooks, implementing chapter-specific filtering and metadata-aware chunking. The PyPDFDirectoryLoader enabled batch processing of multiple PDFs with page range filtering (pages 1-28 for Chapter 1).

Advanced semantic chunking with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B was implemented with gradient breakpoint detection (threshold 90.0), adaptive buffer sizing, and minimum chunk constraints to prevent over-fragmentation. The chunking process removed newlines and normalized whitespace to improve retrieval consistency.

Question Format Enhancement:

Phase 3 introduced more sophisticated question structures with explicit delimiter markers ("Question N Start" and "Question N End") for robust parsing. Rubric formatting was standardized with criterion names, detailed descriptions, and explicit mark allocations (e.g., "[Rubric 1 Start]Criterion 1: Identification and Explanation - (2 marks)").

A parsing function using regular expressions extracted structured question data including question number, question text, rubric entries with criterion names, descriptions, and marks. This enabled automated analysis of rubric complexity and mark distribution.

Comparative Analysis:

Phase 3 included comprehensive comparative analysis documented in the Srip Analysis folder with PDF reports covering:

- Absolute Detailed Analysis of RAG Configuration Variations
- Analysis of RAG Method Context Changes and Impact
- Better Reranking Models for Question Generation
- Chunk Count Versus Performance in Retrieval-Augmented Systems
- Comparative Analysis of Educational Question Generation Approaches
- Comprehensive Analysis of RAG Configuration Variations

These analyses systematically evaluated the impact of different configurations on question quality, retrieval accuracy, generation consistency, and computational efficiency.

Answer Generation System:

An answer generation notebook was developed to automatically generate comprehensive answers to generated questions using the RAG system. This component completes the full cycle from question generation to answer provision, enabling creation of complete assessment materials including questions, rubrics, ideal answers, and student variations.

CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This three-week internship successfully developed and validated a comprehensive Retrieval-Augmented Generation system for automated educational question generation and evaluation, demonstrating significant potential for transforming educational assessment practices. The project systematically progressed through three distinct phases, each building upon previous insights to create an increasingly sophisticated and capable system.

The research established that RAG-enhanced question generation substantially outperforms standalone LLM approaches in terms of contextual accuracy, relevance to course materials, and alignment with pedagogical standards. The systematic exploration of multiple retrieval strategies, chunking methods, and model configurations provided valuable insights into optimal system architecture for educational applications. The HyDE retrieval technique combined with semantic chunking emerged as the superior approach for educational content, producing questions that accurately reflected course material while maintaining appropriate difficulty and conceptual coherence.

The evaluation framework demonstrated that automated assessment of student responses is feasible when grounded in well-defined rubrics and equipped with multiple evaluation strategies. The system successfully generated diverse student response variations spanning the full quality spectrum, providing valuable data for calibrating evaluation systems and training teaching assistants. The comparison of clarification-based and target-guided evaluation strategies revealed important insights about assessment fairness and the role of interpretive leniency in academic evaluation.

The project generated substantial datasets including over 100 high-quality examination questions across multiple computer science domains, comprehensive rubrics with detailed mark allocations, ideal answers demonstrating expected response quality, and 600+ student response variations for evaluation training and validation. These datasets represent valuable resources for future research in educational AI and assessment automation.

Key technical achievements include the implementation of sophisticated document processing pipelines handling complex PDF structures, advanced semantic chunking that preserves conceptual coherence, efficient vector database implementations supporting rapid similarity search, robust LLM orchestration managing multiple models and API endpoints, and comprehensive error handling ensuring system reliability despite non-deterministic AI outputs.

Several challenges were encountered and successfully addressed during the internship. JSON parsing inconsistencies in LLM outputs required development of robust cleaning and error recovery mechanisms. Balancing retrieval chunk count with context window limitations necessitated careful optimization of chunk size and retrieval parameters. Ensuring question diversity while maintaining quality required experimentation with temperature settings and prompt variations. Managing computational resources for embedding generation and LLM inference demanded efficient batching and caching strategies.

The internship significantly contributed to professional growth in multiple dimensions. Technical expertise advanced substantially through hands-on experience with cutting-edge AI technologies, systematic experimentation methodologies, and production-quality software development practices. Domain knowledge deepened through intensive engagement with educational content and assessment theory. Problem-solving abilities enhanced through debugging complex AI systems and optimizing multi-component architectures. Communication skills improved through documentation of complex technical work and explanation of AI concepts to diverse audiences.

Looking forward, several promising directions for future work emerged from this research. Extending the system to additional subject domains beyond computer science would validate its generalizability and identify domain-specific optimization opportunities. Implementing advanced reranking models could further improve retrieval quality, particularly for nuanced conceptual questions. Developing fine-tuned models specifically for educational question generation might improve output quality and reduce computational requirements. Creating interactive interfaces for instructors to guide and refine question generation would increase practical utility. Integrating student performance analytics to adaptively generate questions targeting specific learning gaps would enable personalized assessment.

The broader implications of this work extend beyond the immediate technical achievements. For educational institutions, this technology offers potential to significantly reduce instructor workload in assessment preparation while maintaining or improving quality standards. For students, automated generation of practice questions and immediate feedback on responses can enhance learning opportunities. For the field of educational technology, this research contributes valuable insights into practical applications of Large Language Models and RAG architectures in educational contexts.

In conclusion, this internship successfully demonstrated that AI-assisted educational assessment is not only technically feasible but can achieve quality standards suitable for practical deployment in educational settings. The systematic methodology, comprehensive experimentation, and rigorous evaluation established a solid foundation for future research and development in this important area. The skills acquired, insights gained, and systems developed during this internship represent valuable contributions to both personal professional development and the broader field of educational AI. Most importantly, the work validated the potential for AI technologies to augment rather than replace human expertise in education, supporting instructors in delivering high-quality, scalable, and consistent educational assessment while maintaining the pedagogical rigor essential for effective learning.
