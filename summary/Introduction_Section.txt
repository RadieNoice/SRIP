INTRODUCTION
Development and Evaluation of RAG-Enhanced Question Generation System 
for Educational Assessment
================================================================================

Alignment: Justified


The landscape of educational technology has undergone profound transformation over the past decade, driven by exponential advances in artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and machine learning capabilities. Educational institutions worldwide face mounting pressure to deliver high-quality, personalized learning experiences to increasingly diverse and expanding student populations while simultaneously managing resource constraints and maintaining rigorous academic standards. This challenge is particularly acute in the domain of educational assessment, where the creation of comprehensive, fair, and pedagogically sound evaluation instruments remains fundamentally labor-intensive despite technological advances in nearly every other aspect of academic administration.

This internship was conducted as part of a structured research initiative exploring the practical applications of Large Language Models and Retrieval-Augmented Generation architectures in educational contexts. The research program, spanning approximately three intensive weeks, focused specifically on developing automated systems capable of generating examination-quality questions, comprehensive evaluation rubrics, and diverse student response variations while maintaining the pedagogical rigor essential for valid educational assessment. The internship provided a unique opportunity to bridge theoretical advances in natural language processing with practical challenges faced daily by educators and academic institutions.

The motivation for this project emerged from multiple converging observations in contemporary educational practice. Faculty members across disciplines consistently report that assessment development represents one of their most time-consuming responsibilities, typically requiring 15-20 hours per semester for comprehensive question bank creation. This substantial time investment diverts attention from high-value activities such as curriculum innovation, personalized student mentoring, and research advancement. Moreover, assessment quality exhibits significant variability based on individual instructor expertise, available preparation time, and pedagogical training, leading to potential inequities when multiple instructors teach parallel sections of the same course. The challenge intensifies further when considering the need for diverse question variations to prevent academic dishonesty, the requirement for detailed rubrics that align with learning outcomes, and the necessity of generating realistic student response examples for teaching assistant training and evaluation system calibration.

Recent breakthroughs in transformer-based language models, particularly systems like GPT, Llama, Mistral, and DeepSeek, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding context, generating coherent text, and reasoning about complex topics. However, standalone Large Language Models suffer from well-documented limitations including hallucination of factual information, outdated knowledge resulting from static training data, and insufficient grounding in specific domain contexts. Retrieval-Augmented Generation addresses these fundamental limitations by combining the generative power of LLMs with dynamic information retrieval from authoritative document collections, thereby grounding model outputs in verifiable source materials and dramatically improving factual accuracy and contextual relevance.

This project specifically investigated how RAG architectures could be optimized for educational question generation by systematically exploring document processing strategies, text chunking algorithms, embedding model selection, retrieval techniques, and generation parameters. The research progressed through three distinct phases: foundational question generation using standalone LLMs to establish baseline performance, RAG-enhanced generation incorporating multiple optimization strategies including HyDE retrieval and semantic chunking, and advanced agentic approaches featuring autonomous reasoning loops and dynamic tool selection. The domain focus encompassed Computer Science education at the university level, specifically theoretical subjects including Operating Systems, Data Structures and Algorithms, Cryptography and Network Security, and Software Engineering. These subjects were selected because they represent core computer science curricula with substantial conceptual knowledge requiring assessment beyond simple programming exercises, making them ideal testbeds for evaluating automated question generation quality and pedagogical appropriateness.

The broader significance of this work extends beyond immediate technical achievements. For educational technology companies developing learning management systems and assessment platforms, this research provides validated methodologies for integrating AI capabilities while maintaining educational integrity. For academic institutions, the potential to reduce assessment preparation workload by 60-70% while maintaining or exceeding quality standards represents transformative operational efficiency. For students, access to unlimited, contextually relevant practice questions with immediate feedback creates enhanced learning opportunities. Most importantly, this work demonstrates that artificial intelligence can augment rather than replace human educational expertise, supporting instructors in delivering scalable, consistent, high-quality assessment while preserving the pedagogical judgment and domain knowledge that remain uniquely human contributions to the educational process.
